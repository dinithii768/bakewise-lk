TITLE: Food Act Compliance for Small Food Businesses in Sri Lanka
SOURCE: Ministry of Health Sri Lanka | Food Act No. 26 of 1980

OVERVIEW
Any business in Sri Lanka that produces, processes, sells or distributes food
must comply with the Food Act No. 26 of 1980 and its amendments.

KEY REQUIREMENTS FOR HOME FOOD SELLERS

1. Food Registration
   - Home food businesses may need to register food products with the
     relevant authority depending on the type and scale of production
   - Large scale manufacturers must register with the Food Control Administration Unit

2. PHI Inspection
   - Public Health Inspectors (PHI) under local authorities
     conduct inspections of food premises
   - Home kitchens used for commercial production may be subject to inspection
   - Maintaining cleanliness and hygiene standards is mandatory

3. Food Handler Health
   - Food handlers must be free from communicable diseases
   - It is good practice to maintain food handler health records
   - Hand washing facilities must be available in food preparation areas

4. Storage and Temperature
   - Perishable foods must be stored at correct temperatures
   - Separation of raw and cooked items is required
   - Proper labeling on stored items including preparation dates

5. Water Supply
   - Only clean, safe, potable water should be used in food preparation

PENALTIES
Non-compliance with the Food Act can result in:
- Fines
- Seizure of products
- Business closure orders
- Criminal prosecution in serious cases

PRACTICAL TIPS FOR HOME BAKERS
- Keep your kitchen clean and organized at all times
- Store ingredients properly in sealed containers
- Keep a simple record of ingredients used and production dates
- Invite a PHI visit voluntarily before a surprise inspection
