TITLE: Basic Costing Worksheet for Home Food Businesses
SOURCE: General SME Financial Advisory

OVERVIEW
Understanding your real costs is essential for running a profitable
home food business. Many home sellers lose money because they do not
account for all expenses properly.

FULL COST BREAKDOWN

DIRECT COSTS (change with every batch)
- Raw ingredients (flour, sugar, eggs, butter, flavoring)
- Packaging materials (boxes, bags, labels, ribbon)
- Delivery costs if you deliver personally (fuel, vehicle wear)

INDIRECT COSTS (fixed monthly regardless of orders)
- Electricity estimate for baking and refrigeration
- Water usage
- Internet and phone for taking orders
- Kitchen equipment replacement and maintenance
- Insurance if applicable

YOUR OWN LABOR
- Time spent planning and ordering ingredients
- Time spent baking and decorating
- Time spent packaging and labeling
- Time spent communicating with customers and managing orders
- Time spent on delivery

EXAMPLE MONTHLY COST SUMMARY
Monthly indirect costs: LKR 5,000
Your target labor income per month: LKR 30,000
Batches produced per month: 20 batches

Fixed cost to cover per batch = (5000 + 30000) divided by 20 = LKR 1,750

If one batch produces 12 units:
Fixed cost per unit = 1,750 divided by 12 = LKR 146

Add direct cost per unit (ingredients and packaging): LKR 150
Total cost per unit = LKR 296

Add profit margin of 35 percent: LKR 296 x 1.35 = LKR 400

Selling price per unit: LKR 400

REVIEW YOUR COSTS REGULARLY
- Check ingredient prices monthly as they change with market conditions
- Adjust prices when input costs increase significantly
- Track actual versus estimated costs for every batch
