TITLE: Success Tips for Home Food Businesses in Sri Lanka
SOURCE: General Entrepreneurship Advisory | SME Success Patterns

OVERVIEW
Many successful food businesses in Sri Lanka started from home kitchens.
Here are proven principles that help home food sellers grow sustainably.

TOP SUCCESS PRINCIPLES

1. Focus on One or Two Signature Products First
   - Do not try to sell everything at once
   - Master a few items before expanding your menu
   - Customers remember you for your speciality

2. Consistency is Everything
   - Every product must taste the same as the last
   - Use standardized recipes with measured quantities
   - Keep notes on any changes that improve results

3. Packaging Reflects Your Brand
   - Invest in neat, professional looking packaging
   - A well-presented box creates a premium impression
   - Branded labels build recognition over time

4. Price Correctly from the Start
   - Do not underprice to attract customers
   - Cheap prices attract customers who will not stay loyal
   - Fair pricing for quality products attracts the right customers

5. Manage Your Cash Flow
   - Keep business and personal money completely separate
   - Collect payment before or on delivery whenever possible
   - Keep a simple daily record of income and spending

6. Build a Loyal Customer Base
   - Regular customers are more valuable than one-time buyers
   - Loyalty discounts or a simple stamp card encourages repeat orders
   - Remember preferences of regular customers

7. Comply With Regulations From Day One
   - Surprise inspections happen
   - Legal compliance protects your business
   - Proper registration opens access to loans and contracts

8. Learn and Improve Continuously
   - Take feedback seriously and act on it
   - Follow food trends relevant to your market
   - Attend free training offered by SMEDA and other agencies

COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID
- Accepting more orders than you can fulfill
- Mixing personal and business finances
- Ignoring complaints from customers
- Not labeling allergens on products
- Expanding too fast before systems are ready
